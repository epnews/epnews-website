# LNG domestic-price counterfactual: DOE model versus independent RFF critique

**Evidence date:** 2024 DOE study, March 4 2025 RFF issue brief, and RFF working paper updated May 14 2026. This package adds a previously missing **published domestic-cost counterfactual with sensitivity**. It does **not** establish 2026 realized household costs or net national benefit.

## A. DOE primary-model matched scenario (2050, 2022-dollar prices)
Source: [DOE LNG Export Study summary, Table 10, p. S-27](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/LNGUpdate_SummaryReport_Dec2024_12pm.pdf) and [DOE Appendix B](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/LNGUpdate_AppendixB_Dec2024.pdf).

Under *Defined Policies*, reference US gas supply, **Existing/FID Exports 23.7 Bcf/day** versus **Model Resolved 56.3 Bcf/day**, a **32.6 Bcf/day modeled export difference**:
- Henry Hub: **$3.53 -> $4.62/MMBtu** in 2022 dollars; **+$1.09/MMBtu**, approximately **+31%**.
- Average delivered natural-gas price differences in $/MMBtu: power **+$0.64**, industrial **+$0.78**, residential **+$0.50**, commercial **+$0.48**, transportation **+$0.76**.
- DOE describes modeled 2050 residential prices as **4% higher** and industrial and power-sector delivered prices as **18% higher** in reference supply. These are sectoral price comparisons, not 2026 bill impacts.
- DOE's **high supply** sensitivity: Henry Hub +$0.94, power +$0.46, industrial +$0.64, residential +$0.36, commercial +$0.35, transportation +$0.51.
- DOE's **low supply** sensitivity: Henry Hub +$2.30, power +$1.25, industrial +$1.67, residential +$1.13, commercial +$1.10, transportation +$2.39.
- All three sensitivity rows assume the same +32.6 Bcf/day difference; **supply response**, not a new export volume, drives the different price outcomes.

## B. Independent RFF critique and competing sensitivity
Source: [RFF, Prest, Krupnick, Wingenroth, March 4 2025](https://www.rff.org/publications/issue-briefs/unpacking-the-department-of-energys-report-on-us-liquefied-natural-gas-exports/) and [Prest working paper, updated May 14 2026](https://www.rff.org/publications/working-papers/where-does-the-marginal-methane-molecule-come-from-implications-of-lng-exports-for-us-natural-gas-supply-and-methane-emissions/).

RFF converts DOE's 31% / 32.6 Bcf/day to approximately **0.95% Henry Hub price increase per added Bcf/day** (a descriptive average over a large scenario change). RFF's separate supply-response analysis suggests about **2.5% per Bcf/day**, more than twice DOE's rate. RFF points to DOE's implicitly high gas supply elasticity (~1), versus a long-run supply elasticity below 0.3 in RFF's analysis and an illustrative demand elasticity near -0.2. **The 2.5% is RFF's model-based estimate, not a measured 2026 coefficient and not a validated linear rate over all 32.6 Bcf/day.**

**Crucial disagreement:** DOE's own low-supply sensitivity (+$2.30/MMBtu) is over twice its reference +$1.09, supporting the conclusion that domestic cost incidence is sensitive to supply assumptions. RFF is not an independent empirical observation of actual 2026 household prices. Its critique should be presented alongside DOE's model and DOE's later official Response to Comments, which reached a more favorable policy conclusion: [DOE response](https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-finalizes-2024-lng-export-study-paving-way-stronger-american-energy-exports).

## C. Model applicability and evidence boundary
These projections compare alternative **2050** export levels, *not* a 2026 no-Hormuz-shock scenario. Applying +$0.03/MMBtu per Bcf/day or 2.5% per Bcf/day to the January–October **2026 forecast revision** would combine different vintages, demand regimes, elasticities, horizons and identification strategies. **Do not perform this extrapolation.**

Neither study provides observed 2026 realized export cash receipts, gas utility billing, industrial cost incidence, state-level customer exposure, or a defensible shock-isolated 2026 no-export counterfactual. The studies offer scenario sensitivity bounds and mechanisms, **not a measured 2026 net-benefit number**.

### Specific next-stage dataset join
- Obtain EIA 2026 monthly Henry Hub prices and delivered residential/industrial/electric prices, preferably Gulf Coast versus non-Gulf regions.
- Pair with 2026 EIA supply, production, storage, heating/cooling degree days, pipeline constraints and LNG terminal utilization, with consistent months and price definitions.
- Compare against **archived pre-March 2026 monthly forecasts**, not just annual-average revisions.
- Apply published structural elasticities only as **explicit sensitivity scenarios**, and never represent a modeled difference as realized 2026 household burden.
- Keep exporter revenue, national GDP, taxes, environmental costs, household transfers and export margins as **separate accounts** to avoid double counting.

## D. Readiness decision
