# LNG domestic-cost investigation: observed 2026 gas-market constraints

**New evidence versus PR #257:** PR #257 studied modeled **2050** gas-price impacts; this audit uses **observed 2026** Henry Hub summer prices, production, consumption, exports and inventory context. It does not reprint the modeled scenarios.

## A. Observed June–August price, not a forecast
[EIA, September 25 2026](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=68204): Henry Hub **spot** natural-gas price averaged **$2.93/MMBtu June–August 2026**, **6% below** the comparable 2025 summer period, despite exceptional summer heat. EIA cites increased renewable generation, record natural-gas production, ample storage and LNG facility maintenance as factors limiting price pressure. This is a wholesale benchmark, not a retail residential or industrial delivered price. The 6% is EIA's published comparison, not a reconstructed monthly-price series.

## B. August 2026 physical supply and demand
[EIA October 2026 Natural Gas Monthly, August highlights](https://www.eia.gov/naturalgas/monthly/), preliminary figures:
| Measure | August 2025 | August 2026 | Year-over-year change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| US dry gas production, Bcf/day | 108.65 | 115.84 | +7.19 (+6.6%) |
| US gas consumption, Bcf/day | 85.39 | 90.65 | +5.26 (+6.2%) |
| US LNG exports, Bcf/day | Not separately quoted in this table | **16.61** | **+14.2%** as EIA reports |
| US total gas exports, Bcf/day (includes pipeline) | 24.58 | 27.13 | +2.55 (+10.4%) |
| US net gas exports, Bcf/day | Not separately quoted here | **19.58** | — |

Do **not** equate total natural-gas exports (pipeline plus LNG) with LNG-only exports. The August 2026 LNG exports of 16.61 Bcf/day and August DOE vessel LNG 514.5 Bcf / 31 days = 16.60 Bcf/day (rounding differences) are **cross-source consistency checks**, not independent evidence of shock attribution. They use related export-reporting pipelines and cannot count twice.

## C. Weather and inventory controls, not ex post causation
[EIA September 25](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=68204) reports July Lower-48 average temperature **77°F**, the warmest July in its record, and April 2026 injection-season opening storage **1,906 Bcf**, **4% above** the preceding five-year average. [EIA October 6 STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/marketreview/natgas.php) estimates September-end storage **3,523 Bcf** and forecasts October-end **3,850 Bcf**, **2% above** five-year average. **September-end inventory is an estimate and October-end inventory is a forecast**, not realized October measurement. EIA's October STEO forecasts annual Henry Hub **$3.48/MMBtu in 2026 versus $3.53 in 2025**, and dry gas production **112.20 Bcf/day in 2026 versus 107.64 in 2025**. These annual figures mix historical months and future estimates.

## D. Testable economic conclusion
The observed June–August **wholesale** benchmark **fell 6% year over year**, even as August LNG export volumes were higher. This contradicts any claim that higher LNG exports necessarily coincided with a higher *observed summer Henry Hub spot price*. It does **not** disprove a positive *marginal export effect* relative to a hypothetical no-extra-export world: prices could be lower than last year yet higher than their unobserved counterfactual. Likewise, higher output may offset export demand, and weather/storage/outages confound comparisons.

**Missing to close domestic-cost gate:** matched monthly residential/industrial delivered gas prices, utility billing and volumes; pre-March forecast baseline; weather, production, storage, maintenance and region-level price controls; identification of incremental LNG demand caused by the crisis. Do not multiply modeled 2050 elasticities by 2026 forecast revisions. No 2026 household cost estimate or national net benefit is supported.
