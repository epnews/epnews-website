# US LNG 2026: pre-disruption forecast revision audit

## Newly identified measurable comparison
[EIA April 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook, Natural Gas: LNG exports](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf?stream=top) explicitly reports its **January 2026** full-year LNG export forecast of **16.4 Bcf/day**, and its **April 2026** forecast of **17.0 Bcf/day**. This is a **+0.6 Bcf/day (+3.66%) forecast revision** for the same calendar year and flow definition. It is **not** a measurement of additional realized exports or a causal estimate of exports induced by the March 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption.

EIA states that March exports were **17.9 Bcf/day**, **8% above the January STEO March forecast**; EIA also attributes increased incentives to wider domestic/international price spreads amid disruptions, while noting export capacity constraints and possible maintenance deferral. The April report estimates terminal utilization slightly higher than 2025 and near maximum. The **8%** is a comparison with the January forecast for **March**, not the +3.66% revision in the **full-year** mean; the two must never be equated or added.

| Forecast vintage | Target period | Forecast LNG exports | Interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| January 2026 STEO, quoted in April 2026 STEO | Calendar 2026 average | 16.4 Bcf/day | Pre-disruption forecast baseline |
| April 2026 STEO | Calendar 2026 average | 17.0 Bcf/day | Post-disruption revised forecast |
| Difference | Same calendar 2026 | +0.6 Bcf/day | Forecast revision, **not** observed shock-caused volume |
| April 2026 STEO | March 2026 actual/estimated | 17.9 Bcf/day | 8% above January **March** forecast, distinct comparison |

**Arithmetic:** 17.0 - 16.4 = 0.6 Bcf/day; 100 × 0.6/16.4 = 3.6585...%. Annualizing the *forecast revision only* would be 219 Bcf in a 365-day year **if** the full-year mean revision materialized; this is not observed exports and should not be published as incremental actual cargoes.

## Causal identification: unresolved
A forecast revision can reflect revised plant startup dates, throughput, weather, outages, contract economics, supply and analyst assumptions as well as geopolitical disruptions. EIA's narrative links international disruption to incentives and a possible maintenance deferral, but does not isolate the 0.6 Bcf/day revision as a disruption treatment effect. PR #252 already documents the precommitted project baseline; this new comparison adds a **forecast-vintage benchmark**, not a second project-timing study.

For a defensible causal estimate, compare facility-month realized liftings with archived pre-March 2026 facility-month forecasts, reconcile changes in startup schedules and maintenance, and identify diversion from non-US suppliers. A January-vintage annual forecast is a useful constraint but is not a controlled counterfactual.

## Cross-check against realized H1 outcome
[EIA September 1, 2026](https://www.eia.gov/TodayinEnergy/detail.php?id=68064) reports first-half actual exports averaging **17.4 Bcf/day** (+23% versus first-half 2025). This H1 actual is **not directly comparable** to either January or April full-year 2026 forecast as a forecast error, because the periods differ. No unqualified 'actual minus forecast' figure should be displayed.

## Decision
This audit closes **a missing vintage-to-vintage forecast comparison** while leaving the national-benefit and causal shock-attribution gates open. Existing revenue/FOB and capacity research is referenced rather than duplicated. No net economic benefit, influence score, or readiness uplift is justified. **53% readiness remains; national benefit and influence score null.** No dashboard code changed.
