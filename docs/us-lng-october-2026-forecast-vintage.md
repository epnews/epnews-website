# October 2026 EIA LNG forecast vintage: new matched-year comparison

This is a **new October forecast vintage**, not a repeat of PR #253's January-to-April research. Source: archived EIA *Short-Term Energy Outlook* Table 5a, **LNG gross exports (d)**, annual **Year 2026** column:
- [January 2026 STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/jan26.pdf): **16.4 Bcf/day**.
- [April 2026 STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/apr26.pdf): **17.0 Bcf/day**.
- [October 2026 STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/oct26.pdf): **17.6 Bcf/day**.

| Forecast vintage | Forecast calendar-year 2026 gross LNG exports | Change versus January | Change versus April |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January 2026 | 16.4 Bcf/day | baseline | — |
| April 2026 | 17.0 Bcf/day | +0.6 Bcf/day (+3.66%) | baseline |
| October 2026 | 17.6 Bcf/day | **+1.2 Bcf/day (+7.32%)** | **+0.6 Bcf/day (+3.53%)** |

All values are rounded to one decimal by EIA; percentage calculations use those published rounded figures. These are *annual-average forecasts*, not realized 2026 full-year export volumes. The October 2026 vintage incorporates more actual months and updated assumptions; it is not a pure independent observation of an exogenous disruption.

## Added evidence: same October forecast's quarterly distribution
EIA October 2026 Table 5a estimates **2026 Q1 17.8**, **Q2 17.2**, **Q3 17.0**, and **Q4 18.2 Bcf/day**. The 2026 annual 17.6 is EIA's published figure; simple averaging of four rounded quarter means also gives 17.55, which rounds to 17.6 under standard half-up rounding. **Do not substitute quarterly forecast numbers for final measured monthly exports** or infer that a Q4 rebound proves a political shock benefit.

## What this resolves
A third, newer, period-matched forecast benchmark now exists. January-to-October full-year 2026 forecast revisions sum to +1.2 Bcf/day, split +0.6 by April and +0.6 by October. **This is a forecast revision, not a causal 1.2 Bcf/day shock effect.** Revision drivers can include terminal commissioning, outages, maintenance, price spreads, cargo diversion, demand and changes in assumptions. The previous PR #252 capacity commitment and #253 April forecast audits remain valid and are not duplicated here.

To close the remaining causal gate, reconcile forecast-vintage monthly trajectories with terminal-month liftings, committed capacity, outages and credible no-shock price/demand assumptions. Do not annualize the forecast revision into observed cargoes, profits, taxes or national benefit. **Readiness remains 53%; net national benefit null; influence score null.** No dashboard code changed.
