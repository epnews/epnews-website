# Why the five diplomatic milestones are not yet one influence metric

All five cases have **within-case descriptive baseline/follow-up observations**, but the units measure different things. A common diplomatic influence needle requires validated attribution, costs, normalization and comparable outcomes—not just a completed source checklist.

| Sphere | Observable metric | Unit class | Cross-sphere score ready? |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | binary institutional operational status (preactivation arrangement vs activated continuous sharing) | binary-operational-institutional-status | No |
| CHINA | bilateral diplomatic mission status | binary-diplomatic-mission-status | No |
| RUSSIA | binary legal status of the same bilateral treaty (not-in-force vs in-force) | binary-treaty-legal-status | No |
| EU | EUR nominal financial reference amount for successive approximately two-year mandates of the same EUPM mission | nominal-eur-financial-reference-amount | No |
| INDIA | nominal bilateral merchandise trade USD billion | nominal-usd-bilateral-trade-flow | No |

The US measures activation of information-sharing; China measures restored embassies; Russia measures legal treaty entry into force; the EU reports budget reference amounts; India reports nominal bilateral merchandise trade. These cannot be summed, ranked or normalized by arbitrary min/max scaling. In particular, legal budget authority is not disbursement, and trade growth is not CEPA-caused influence. The EU is a supranational actor and must not silently inherit member-state actions. Keep the original meter's unscored state until independent attribution, comparable outcomes, cost evidence and calibration are completed.
