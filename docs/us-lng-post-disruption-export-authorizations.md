# Post-disruption LNG authorization actions: new policy evidence

**Source:** [IEA Gas Market Report Q3 2026 executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/gas-market-report-q3-2026/executive-summary), section on supply response after Strait of Hormuz closure.

## Newly identified, dated US policy actions

| Facility | Timing | Authorization action reported by IEA | IEA stated authorization increase | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plaquemines LNG | March 2026 | US government authorized increase in exports to FTA and non-FTA countries | **13%, equivalent to 4.6 bcm/year** | **Regulatory permission**, not actual added cargoes or completed capacity |
| Elba Island LNG | Early April 2026 | US government authorized increase in exports to non-FTA countries | **22%, equivalent to 0.8 bcm/year** | **Regulatory permission**, not actual added cargoes or completed capacity |

These actions postdate the March disruption and are distinct from pre-committed terminal construction described in PR #252 and national EIA forecast revisions described in PRs #253–254. The IEA reports them as measures to boost available LNG supply. **Timing and stated policy response are documented**, but neither an authorization nor a higher annual limit establishes a measured increase in physical exports caused by the action. The permissions apply to different authorization categories and facilities; do not sum their percentages. The two stated bcm/year increments total **5.4 bcm/year in authorized ceilings** only, and must not be labeled realized supply or national economic benefit.

## Independent contextual constraint
The same IEA report says Qatar/UAE LNG loadings declined **35 bcm year-on-year March–June 2026**, non-Gulf LNG production increased approximately **27 bcm** year-on-year in that period, and global production decreased approximately **8 bcm**. This is a global supply context, not a US-only export volume or attributable US gain. The 27 bcm non-Gulf increase includes other exporters and cannot be assigned to the US. The IEA notes demand destruction and fuel switching, important counterweights to a simple exporter-benefit narrative.

## Follow-up measurement gate
Find DOE/FERC facility authorization orders and effective dates, compare terminal monthly physical cargoes with permitted limits before/after the action, and account for existing nameplate capacity, maintenance and weather. Determine whether authorization was binding absent the policy change. If the ceiling was nonbinding, observed additional cargoes cannot be credited to it.

## Decision
**New verified action-level evidence**: two post-disruption US regulatory export authorizations, their dates, facilities, scope and annualized ceiling increments. **Not verified**: incremental physical cargoes, causal attribution, exporter realized receipts, US net benefit, or diplomatic concessions. **Command Meter readiness 53%; net national benefit null; geopolitical influence score null.** Existing dashboard code unchanged.
