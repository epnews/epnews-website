# India–UAE CEPA: official observed merchandise trade

Source: Government of India, Lok Sabha answer 3492, 17 December 2024: https://www.commerce.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/LS-USQ-No.3492-dated.-17.12.2024.pdf

CEPA entered into force on 1 May 2022. The official DGCIS table provides exports, imports and total merchandise trade for FY2022–23 and FY2023–24 and matched April–September periods of 2023 and 2024.

| Indicator | FY2022–23 ($m) | FY2023–24 ($m) | Change | Apr–Sep 2023 ($m) | Apr–Sep 2024 ($m) | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| india-uae-exports | 31608.79 | 35625.02 | 12.71% | 15467.63 | 17206.58 | 11.24% |
| india-uae-imports | 53231.55 | 48025.58 | -9.78% | 20695.03 | 31458.27 | 52.01% |
| india-uae-total-merchandise-trade | 84840.34 | 83650.6 | -1.4% | 36162.65 | 48664.85 | 34.57% |

These are **three linked reported categories**, not six independent outcomes. Total trade overlaps exports and imports. Figures are nominal merchandise values, not trade volume, net welfare, government revenue, firm profit, CEPA preference utilization, or influence scores. The official table's total trade values may differ by minor rounding from summing export and import components; preserve reported source values. Earlier official releases and later revisions can differ: this dataset retains a single December 2024 vintage. Growth after the agreement is not proof that the agreement caused the growth. The 2023–24 full-year total actually declined; the following April–September matched comparison rose, mainly on imports. Attribution and cost gates remain open. No needle movement is authorized.
