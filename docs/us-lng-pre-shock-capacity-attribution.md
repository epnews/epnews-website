# LNG pre-shock capacity and 2026 attribution audit

**Question:** Does 2026 US LNG export growth demonstrate capacity created in response to 2026 geopolitical disruption? **No.** Independently documented pre-shock investment and forecasts identify a substantial pre-existing expansion pipeline. This resolves a narrower *project-timing classification*, **not** the remaining quantitative no-shock export counterfactual.

## Pre-shock predictions versus observed 2026 operations

| Project or observation | Dated pre-shock record | Observed 2026 record | Evidentiary conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Golden Pass | FID February **2019**; EIA October **2025** forecast first cargo in **H1 2026** and two trains operating during 2026 | EIA April **23, 2026**: first cargo **April 22** after first production in March; nominal 3-train capacity **2.0 Bcf/d** | Project decision and first-2026-cargo expectation predate 2026 Hormuz disruptions |
| Corpus Christi Stage 3 | EIA October **2025** forecast four remaining trains starting during 2026, adding **0.8 Bcf/d nominal** | EIA September **1, 2026**: six of seven trains exporting | Expansion not evidence of a newly caused 2026 capital commitment |
| Plaquemines Phase 1/2 | EIA October **2025** forecast Phases 1 and 2 adding **2.6 Bcf/d nominal** by end-2025; first cargo Dec 2024, Phase 2 production July 2025 | EIA September **1, 2026**: terminal exporting at full capacity | Existing investment and ramp-up precede disruption |
| Aggregate LNG exports | EIA October **2025** already expected over **5 Bcf/d** additional US capacity across 2025–2026 | EIA September **1, 2026**: H1 exports averaged **17.4 Bcf/d**, **23%** above H1 2025 | Observed growth alone cannot be credited to the 2026 shock |

## What this audit does and does not close
**Verified:** The three named 2025–2026 ramp-up projects were approved, financed or expected before the 2026 disruption. A post-shock rise in US exports cannot automatically be called a shock-created capacity gain.

**Still unmeasured:** Difference between actual 2026 utilization and a credible *no-shock* utilization forecast, matched by facility, train, month, planned outages, global demand and diversion from alternative suppliers. Pre-shock forecasts are a baseline input, not a randomized counterfactual. The 17.4 Bcf/d observation must not be subtracted from an October 2025 capacity *addition* number as though both were export flow levels. Capacity is not shipments, and nominal capacity is not peak capacity.

**Economic and diplomatic scoring:** Neither the existence of capacity nor its timing establishes realized cash receipts, US net benefit, domestic price effects or foreign concessions. The capacity *timing classification* is verified; the causal quantity gate remains open. **Readiness remains 53%; national benefit and influence score remain null.** Existing meter needle and controls unchanged.
