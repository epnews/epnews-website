HOW WE MEASURE THE GLOBAL POWER SHIFT ↓

Scoring gate (published October 2026): Existing GDP, defense expenditure, EU gas volumes and UN voting measures are observable proxies, not direct evidence of changed geopolitical influence. They must not be combined into an overall score. Each dimension requires a fixed year/region baseline, at least two independently sourced outcome measures, beneficiary and payer accounting, plausible alternative explanations, and moderate or high evidence confidence. Diplomatic outcomes require implemented agreements or demonstrated shifts in other actors' behavior; UN voting similarity alone is not sufficient. Confidence and completeness are displayed separately. Scores are approved by editorial review and subjected to sensitivity checks against alternate weights. The EU institutional sphere is distinguished from member-state aggregates and from all European countries. Gas figures use the Council EC/LSEG/ENTSOG series; Bruegel estimates require method reconciliation.

Methodology references: Lowy Institute Asia Power Index (separates capabilities from influence) and SIPRI expenditure definitions (military spending is an input, not military capability).

Current evidence coverage: 20 of 20 actor/dimension records, including an explicitly limited Russian ratification record. Coverage means a cited record exists; it does not mean any dimension is ready for influence scoring. These records have different baselines and units. An overall index is withheld until all four influence dimensions have independently supported outcomes on comparable scales.

EP News measures changes in influence against a dated baseline, not a nation's existing size or power. Scores are editorial indices, not scientific measurements.

DimensionWeightEvidence to review
Economic30%Realized investment, trade, production, jobs and net costs
Strategic/security30%Access agreements, deployments, production capacity, autonomy and costs
Diplomatic20%Agreements implemented, voting coalitions, bargaining leverage and durable policy shifts

Normalization contract: A future reviewed dimension score is 100 × net outcome change ÷ a materiality target fixed before assessment, capped at ±100. Targets must be justified in a published worksheet; none is calibrated or approved in this release. Proxies cannot satisfy the gate. Missing dimensions never redistribute weights. Sensitivity checks use equal weights and economic/strategic 40/20 and 20/40 alternatives. Read the full methodology and remaining gates ↗

Each dimension is reviewed on a −100 to +100 change scale. Composite = 0.30 economic + 0.30 strategic + 0.20 diplomatic + 0.20 energy/resources. Categories must be assessed with dated baseline, credible citations, counterevidence, and documented costs; otherwise no composite score is published. A provisional score is never substituted for missing evidence.

Evidence confidence is independent of direction: High requires multiple independent sources and realized outcomes; Moderate requires a solid primary record with material uncertainties; Low denotes preliminary or incomplete evidence and is not eligible for a public composite score. All changes require editorial review. Rival spheres and individual allied countries can gain simultaneously.

Five presentation zones: strong loss (−100 to −60), mild loss (−59 to −20), no material change (−19 to +19), mild gain (+20 to +59), strong gain (+60 to +100). These thresholds are editorial design conventions, not externally validated statistics.

Editorial safeguard: No numeric influence scores are displayed until every scoring gate is met. Raw measurements are shown in their original units; no proxy is passed off as influence. The coverage gauge does not imply neutral or equal influence.
